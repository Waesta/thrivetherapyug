Getting Started

5 questions
What is therapy and how does it work?

Therapy is a professional, confidential process in which you work with a trained clinician to explore thoughts, feelings, behaviours, and life challenges. Sessions provide a safe, non-judgemental space where you can speak openly, gain insight, develop coping skills, and work towards meaningful change.

At Thrive, our clinician uses evidence-based approaches — including Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT), trauma-informed care, and person-centred counselling — tailored to your unique needs and goals. Progress is collaborative: you set the pace, and your clinician guides and supports you every step of the way.

How do I know if I need therapy?

You do not need to be in crisis to benefit from therapy. Common reasons people seek support include persistent sadness, anxiety, or worry; relationship difficulties; grief or loss; stress at work or school; low self-esteem; trauma; hormonal or physical health concerns; or simply feeling stuck and wanting more from life.

A useful rule of thumb: if something is affecting your sleep, your relationships, your ability to work, or your enjoyment of daily life — it is worth talking to someone. Seeking help is a sign of courage and self-awareness, not weakness.

I've never been to therapy before — what should I expect in the first session?

Your first session is primarily a conversation. Our clinician will take time to understand who you are, what has brought you in, and what you hope to achieve. You will be asked some questions about your background, current situation, and any relevant history — but there is absolutely no pressure to share more than you are comfortable with.

By the end of the session, you will have a clearer picture of how therapy can help you and what a personalised plan might look like. Most people leave their first session feeling heard, relieved, and more hopeful than they expected. It is also completely normal to feel a little nervous beforehand — that is natural and your clinician will put you at ease.

How is Thrive Therapy & Wellness different from other clinics?

Thrive is led by Patience Ahebwa — a Clinician, Public Health Specialist, and Counselling Psychologist with deep roots in Ugandan communities. Several things set us apart:

A whole-person approach: We integrate mental health, hormonal health, physical strength, and lifestyle into a single, coherent care model — not just talk therapy in isolation.

Cultural awareness: We understand Ugandan social, family, and community dynamics. You will never have to over-explain your context or feel misunderstood.

No one-size-fits-all treatment: Every care plan is individually tailored. We focus on root causes rather than masking symptoms.

Accessible and flexible: We offer in-person sessions in Ntinda, Kampala and secure online sessions for clients anywhere in the world.

Do I need a referral from a doctor to book?

No referral is needed. You can book directly by calling us, sending a WhatsApp message, or completing the online booking form. We welcome self-referrals — most of our clients come to us directly.

If your doctor, psychiatrist, or another health professional has referred you, please let us know. We are happy to coordinate care and share progress notes with your referring clinician, with your written consent, to ensure you receive joined-up support.

Confidentiality & Privacy

4 questions
Is everything I share confidential?

Yes — without reservation. Confidentiality is the foundation of effective therapy and it is one of our most strongly held professional obligations. Everything you share in your sessions — your personal history, your thoughts, your feelings, your circumstances — is strictly private and will not be disclosed to anyone without your explicit written consent.

This includes family members, friends, employers, colleagues, other health professionals, and any other third parties. You are in full control of who knows you are in therapy and what, if anything, is shared.

Are there any exceptions to confidentiality?

There are a small number of legal and ethical exceptions where we may be required or professionally obligated to act, even without your consent. These are:

Risk of serious harm: If you disclose credible, imminent intent to harm yourself or another identifiable person, we may need to take protective action — including contacting emergency services.

Court order: If a court of law legally compels disclosure of session records, we are required to comply.

Child or vulnerable adult safeguarding: If there are concerns that a child or vulnerable adult is being abused or is at serious risk, we are obligated to report this to the appropriate authorities.

Your clinician will explain all of these limits clearly in your first session. In practice, these situations are rare, and we will always aim to discuss concerns with you first wherever it is safe to do so.

Will my employer or family know I'm attending therapy?

No. Your attendance at Thrive is completely private. We will not contact your employer, family members, or anyone in your life unless you specifically request this and sign a written release of information form.

Many clients find it helpful to keep therapy private, at least initially, while they settle in and feel ready to share on their own terms. Whether you choose to tell people is entirely your decision. We will never make that choice for you.

How is my personal data stored and protected?

We take the security of your personal information seriously. Client records are accessed only by your clinician. Paper records are kept in locked, secure storage. Digital notes and records are held on password-protected systems with restricted access.

We follow professional ethical guidelines and applicable Ugandan data protection standards. Session notes are kept concise and professionally worded. You have the right to request access to your records at any time, and to ask for any inaccuracies to be corrected. Records are retained for the period required by professional standards and then securely destroyed.

Sessions & Process

6 questions
How long is a therapy session?

A standard individual therapy session is 50–60 minutes. Initial consultation sessions may run slightly longer — up to 75 minutes — to allow a thorough assessment and discussion of your goals and background.

Couples and family therapy sessions are typically 60–75 minutes to give adequate time for all parties to participate. Workplace wellness workshops and group therapy sessions vary depending on the programme design and will be agreed with you or your organisation in advance.

How many sessions will I need?

The number of sessions varies greatly — it depends on what you are working through, your personal goals, your life circumstances, and how you respond to therapy. This is something we take seriously and review with you regularly.

Some clients experience significant relief and clarity in as few as 4–6 sessions — particularly when addressing a specific, time-limited concern. Others benefit from longer-term support over several months, especially when working through complex trauma, deep-rooted patterns, or ongoing life challenges.

After your initial consultation, your clinician will recommend a personalised plan with a suggested number of sessions and a review point. You are always free to continue, pause, or end therapy at any time.

How often should I come?

Most clients begin with weekly sessions. Meeting weekly provides the continuity and momentum that allows meaningful therapeutic work to build from one session to the next. It also helps you and your clinician develop a strong working relationship more quickly.

As you progress and stabilise, sessions may shift to fortnightly or even monthly to support the consolidation of gains and your growing independence. Your clinician will recommend a frequency based on your specific situation, and you can always discuss adjustments at any point.

What is the difference between a consultation and a therapy session?

A consultation (sometimes called an assessment or intake session) is your first appointment. Its primary purpose is for your clinician to understand your background, presenting concerns, personal history, and therapy goals. Think of it as a carefully structured conversation designed to determine the most appropriate form of support for you.

The therapy sessions that follow are where the ongoing, in-depth work happens. These sessions build on each other over time — exploring root causes, processing experiences, developing coping strategies, shifting unhelpful patterns, and tracking meaningful progress towards your goals.

Can I switch therapists or services if needed?

Absolutely. Your comfort, sense of safety, and confidence in the therapeutic relationship are essential to good outcomes. If at any point you feel that your current approach is not working for you, or you would like to explore a different service or format, please feel free to speak openly with your clinician — or contact us directly at info@thriveug.com or by phone.

There is no shame or awkwardness in requesting a change. We will always work in your best interest, and finding the right fit is a shared priority.

What happens if I need to cancel or reschedule?

We ask that you give at least 24 hours' notice if you need to cancel or reschedule your appointment. This courtesy allows us to offer your slot to another client who may be waiting for support.

Late cancellations (fewer than 24 hours' notice) or missed appointments without any notice may be subject to a cancellation fee. We understand, however, that life can be unpredictable — emergencies happen. If you are facing a genuine emergency, please contact us as soon as you are able and we will do our best to accommodate you with understanding and flexibility.

You can reach us to reschedule by calling +256 775 939 636 or via WhatsApp.

Online & In-Person

4 questions
Do you offer online therapy sessions?

Yes. We offer fully secure, confidential video therapy sessions for clients who prefer online access or who are unable to attend in person. Online sessions are available to clients throughout Uganda and to international clients worldwide.

You can book an online session in exactly the same way as an in-person appointment — via WhatsApp, phone call, or the online booking form. Simply select "Online (Video Call)" as your preferred format when booking.

Is online therapy as effective as in-person?

Yes. A substantial and growing body of research consistently demonstrates that online therapy (also called teletherapy or e-therapy) is equally effective as face-to-face therapy for most mental health concerns — including anxiety, depression, stress, grief, trauma, and relationship difficulties.

What matters most in therapy is the quality of the therapeutic relationship, the skill of your clinician, and your engagement in the process — not the physical medium. Many clients actually find it easier to open up from the privacy and comfort of their own home or office. Others appreciate the time and cost savings of removing travel.

What do I need for an online session?

Getting set up for an online session is straightforward. You will need:

A device: A smartphone, tablet, or computer with a working camera and microphone.

A stable internet connection: A reliable mobile data or Wi-Fi connection. You do not need very high speed — a standard 4G connection is typically sufficient.

A private space: A quiet, private location where you will not be interrupted or overheard for the duration of your session. This is important for your comfort and confidentiality.

We will agree on the platform that works best for you — options include WhatsApp Video, Google Meet, or Zoom. Your clinician will send you a link or call you at the agreed time. No special software downloads are required for WhatsApp or Google Meet.

I live outside Kampala / outside Uganda — can I still access your services?

Yes, absolutely. Our online therapy service is available to clients throughout Uganda — whether you are in Gulu, Mbarara, Jinja, Fort Portal, or any other location — and to Ugandan diaspora and international clients worldwide.

We have supported clients across East Africa, Europe, North America, and beyond. We are experienced in navigating different time zones and will find a session time that works for you. Please contact us to discuss scheduling and payment options, as some international payment methods may differ from our standard process.

Services & Specialties

5 questions
What mental health conditions do you treat?

Our clinician has broad experience supporting individuals across a wide range of concerns, including but not limited to:

Mood & anxiety: Depression, anxiety disorders, panic, phobias, OCD, and persistent low mood.
Trauma & stress: PTSD, complex trauma, burnout, grief, and bereavement.
Relationships & life transitions: Relationship difficulties, divorce, identity concerns, career transitions, and major life change.
Addictions: Substance use, alcohol dependence, and behavioural addictions.
Physical health: Chronic illness, HIV/AIDS counselling, sexual and reproductive health, and fertility-related distress.
Academic & career: Exam stress, academic underperformance, workplace conflict, and career burnout.

If you are unsure whether your concern falls within our scope, please reach out — we will be honest about whether we can help or whether a referral elsewhere would serve you better.

Do you offer couples and family therapy?

Couples therapy helps partners understand each other more deeply, communicate more effectively, resolve conflict constructively, and rebuild or strengthen intimacy and trust. We work with couples at all stages — dating, engaged, married, or co-parenting — and across different types of relationships.

Family therapy brings family members together to address patterns of conflict, improve communication, support a member who is struggling, or navigate a shared crisis such as bereavement, illness, or significant change. We work with family systems of all sizes and structures, including single-parent families and blended families.

Both services are available in person in Ntinda and online. Please book a session and indicate the service type so we can prepare appropriately.

What is hormonal health support?

Hormones have a profound effect on mood, energy, cognition, weight, sleep, and overall wellbeing — yet the emotional and psychological impact of hormonal conditions is frequently overlooked in standard medical care.

Hormonal health support at Thrive addresses the full impact of conditions such as PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome), perimenopause, menopause, thyroid conditions, and related hormonal imbalances. Our approach combines counselling to address emotional challenges, education to help you understand what is happening in your body, nutritional and lifestyle guidance, and practical tools to help you manage symptoms and regain a sense of control and vitality.

You do not need a formal diagnosis to access this support. If hormonal changes are affecting your quality of life, we can help.

Do you provide workplace wellness programmes?

Yes. We partner with organisations, schools, NGOs, businesses, and institutions across Uganda to design and deliver tailored workplace mental health and wellness programmes. We recognise that employee wellbeing is directly linked to productivity, retention, and organisational culture.

Our workplace offerings include: mental health awareness workshops; stress management and resilience training; burnout prevention programmes; leadership mental health coaching; team wellbeing assessments; and Employee Assistance Programme (EAP) counselling support.

All programmes are customised to your organisation's size, sector, and specific needs. Please contact us to discuss a proposal — we would be delighted to explore how we can support your team.

Do you work with children and teenagers?

Yes. We work with adolescents and young people facing a range of challenges including academic pressure and exam stress, bullying, anxiety, depression, family conflict, grief and loss, relationship difficulties, and identity or self-esteem concerns.

For younger adolescents, we typically involve parents or primary caregivers as part of a family-centred approach — both to ensure safety and to maximise the impact of sessions. For older teenagers, individual sessions with appropriate parental consent may be more suitable.

Every young person's situation is different. Please reach out to us to describe your child's specific situation and we will advise on the most appropriate and effective approach.

Session fees are discussed transparently during the booking process before you commit to anything. We are committed to making quality mental health care as accessible as possible, and we aim to be clear and straightforward about costs from the start — no hidden fees.
